## Issues by owner

### env

- **[blocker]** *03_golden / 08_golden_world_only, meadow y 1150-2400 (1080x2400 coords), worst below the brook y 2000-2400 and the right edge x 950-1080 y 1500-2100* — The golden meadow goes to a muddy brown-olive (it looks close to L* 40-45, not the 54-60 the bible asks for), and the shadows sit on it as near-black blobs. Behind the stump, under the sun stone and in the right foreground they read as dark holes, not long warm shadows. This is the signature frame, and its ground has the worst values in the set. V2 fails at 80.2 % world-only and 84.6 % with UI.
  - **Fix:** In meadow.gdshader, lift the golden ground toward #AD8773 at the crest and #977B73 at the brook by checking the ambient multiply. It looks applied twice, once to the ground and once through the contact shadow. Make the contact and long shadows a tint, mix(ground, shadow_tint #7A5F6E, 0.35), not a multiply toward black. Cap shadow alpha at 0.35 and widen the blur along the long axis so that 2.5x-length shadows fade out instead of ending in a hard dark blob.
- **[blocker]** *31_lineup_golden_both (all 16 creatures), also 30_lineup_day_both* — The long golden shadows fan up and away from the viewer, toward the sun, behind every creature. Bible §1.2 says golden shadows fall toward the viewer and fan away from the sun. The lineup scene disagrees with the main scene (03, where the stump and stone shadows fall down the screen), so there are two light directions in one game and the shadow-direction audit fails.
  - **Fix:** Have the lineup fixture read the shared g_sun_dir global and stop overriding it. In contact_shadow.gdshader, compute the offset as -g_sun_dir.xy * length so the shadow is projected away from the sun. With the sun at (560,640) and above the subjects, the shadows should come down the screen. Add a GUT test that checks the sign of the shadow centroid against g_sun_dir in every fixture.
- **[major]** *03_golden focal box, sun stone (540,1123 design / ~650,1560 capture) and the area around it* — The focal hierarchy is weak at golden hour. The stone top is the same value as the haze-washed ground, there is a heavy dark pool under its front lip, and the five creatures, basket and flower bed are all at similar contrast, so nothing wins at a squint. The bright basket and the daisies compete with Puffmoss.
  - **Fix:** Brighten the stone's lit top to #DCB998 and add a key-coloured rim along its sun-facing edge. Drop the lit side of the daisy and clover bed by about 8 L* (multiply fl_* by 0.9 at golden) and the basket by about 6 L*. Keep the strongest value step (fur rim at L* 90+ against stone shadow) on Puffmoss alone, then run the squint check (Gaussian σ = 2 % of width) and confirm the stone box holds the top contrast tile.
- **[major]** *07/08 world-only and 03, foreground below the brook (y 2000-2400), and the empty sky (y 0-800 at golden)* — The bottom fifth of the frame is undesigned: repeated grass stickers on a flat ground, then the L5 frame corners, which are a few dark leaf shapes. Combined with an empty top 35 %, the world sits in a narrow band, and a random 1:1 crop of either end fails the key-art test.
  - **Fix:** Make the L5 foreground frame a deliberate dark, out-of-focus vignette: large blurred fern and grass silhouettes in #5A4A62 on both corners, lifted to about 20 % of the frame height so they frame the brook. At golden, put 2-3 more haze clouds with warm-lit undersides at y 300-700 so the sky carries more of the frame.
- **[major]** *31_lineup_golden_both.png (whole frame), also 03_golden dark smears under the stump (~x 900, y 1450) and under the flower bed (~x 500, y 1750)* — At golden hour the long shadows in the lineup fan upward and diagonally away from the viewer, toward the sun side. This contradicts §1.2 ('fall toward the viewer, fan away from the sun') and the main still, where they fall downward. In the hollow the shadows read as near-black olive-brown smears rather than tinted pools. These shadows were the lighting cue that broke my trust in the scene.
  - **Fix:** In contact_shadow.gdshader, take the projection from the global g_sun_dir with y forced positive (screen-down) so it does not depend on a scene-local anchor. Clamp length to 2.5× object height. Tint the shadow as mix(ground, shade_tint, a) using the palette's derived shadow slot rather than multiplying toward black, and cap alpha at about 0.35 at golden. Add the shadow-direction audit from §11.2 to CI for the lineup scene.
- **[major]** *03_golden / 08_golden_world_only meadow band y 1250-2000* — The golden meadow reads khaki or mud-olive (#8A7A5A-ish) rather than the bible's warm #AD8773 to #977B73. This is why V2 fails (84.6 % with UI, 80.2 % world-only). The signature frame looks dusty rather than glowing next to Alba or Tiny Glade sunsets.
  - **Fix:** In the golden palette in palettes.json, lift meadow L* by about 4 and rotate hue about 8° toward peach. Bring the ambient multiply on grass tufts toward #CA956F tips. Reduce the green component of the golden ambient on L4, or shift the meadow.gdshader golden tint 15 % toward key. Re-run V2 with a target of at least 86 %.
- **[major]** *03_golden, 08_golden_world_only, motion_strip: whole meadow band y 1250-2400, worst in the empty near bank below the brook (y 1950-2400 in 08)* — The signature golden frame looks muddy. The meadow is a dull olive-brown dirt colour, not the peach-lit #AD8773 to #977B73 ground the bible asks for, and the grass looks grey-green. It feels dusty and a bit 'dirty', not warm and glowing. This is why V2 fails at 84.6% with UI and 80.2% world-only, and why golden is the only time of day that loses the cozy read.
  - **Fix:** In palettes.json golden, raise ground_front_lit and the meadow ramp to at least L* 58-60 at the crest and 54 at the brook, and mix them 10% toward key #FFC266 rather than toward shadow. Cut the golden ambient-multiply on L4 so the grass tips reach #CA956F. Re-run metrics.py until V2 is at least 86% on 03 and 08.
- **[major]** *03_golden: dark blotches to the right of the stump (approx x 820-1000, y 1350-1650), under the flower bed (x 430-560, y 1600-1800) and along the right edge; 08 the same* — The long golden shadows render as dark, soft-edged brown-grey smudges that read as stains or holes in the ground, not as tinted light falloff. To a player they add visual noise and a slightly grimy feeling, and they compete with the focal for local contrast.
  - **Fix:** In fx/contact_shadow.gdshader (long-shadow mode), tint toward the golden shadow token (mauve, e.g. mix(shadow, sky_mid, .35)) and not toward black-brown. Cap alpha at 0.30, feather the far end with smoothstep over the last 40% of length, and clamp length to 2.5x object height as §1.2 states.
- **[major]** *31_lineup_golden_both: every creature; compare the stump shadow in 03_golden* — In the golden lineup the shadows fan upward, behind and away from the viewer. In the main scene they fall down and right toward the viewer. That makes two light directions for the same tod, which breaks §1.2 ('shadows fall toward the viewer and fan away from the sun') and the one-light-direction rule.
  - **Fix:** Derive the shadow offset from g_sun_dir in one shared include, with the screen-space sign flipped when the sun is behind the scene (sun_dir.y < 0 maps to +y on screen). Make the lineup scene use the same PaletteDriver globals as Main and stop setting its own.
- **[minor]** *05_night_lanterns and 17_garden_lantern_tap crop: the warm band along the far bank, y 1750-1880* — The lantern light reads as one flat yellow strip with a fairly hard upper edge, not as separate breathing pools of radius 150 px. Individual lanterns lose their 'little warm island' charm, the strongest emotional beat at night.
  - **Fix:** Render one radial halo_add quad per lantern (radius 150 px, #FFC978, alpha 0.25-0.40, falloff exp(-3r^2)) instead of the shared band. Add the ±5% breathing with a per-lantern phase offset so the pools shimmer independently.
### creature

- **[major]** *32_lineup_closeup_day_zoom200 (Pebblepaw face), and 02/03 zoom200 fur bodies* — At 2.2x the fur grain shows stair-stepped blocks of about 4 px, which looks like a low-resolution noise texture sampled with nearest filtering, or a grain that is not anchored to object scale. This is a 200 % edge failure under c3. The silhouette rims and eyes are clean, so this is the only edge rule that breaks.
  - **Fix:** In mossling_fur.gdshader, set the fur noise sampler to filter_linear_mipmap with repeat, and take the grain UV from the creature's local SDF space multiplied by world scale, so the grain density stays constant at any zoom. If the texture is under 256 px, raise it to 512, or generate the fibres procedurally with value noise using smoothstep interpolation.
- **[minor]** *03/08 golden, Honeyhum and Buttonbud on the stone; 31 golden lineup* — The peach-fuzz rim is strong on Puffmoss, but on the warm-furred species (Honeyhum, Primpip, Capling) it almost disappears into the body colour, so the signature backlight is uneven across species.
  - **Fix:** Rim color = mix(#FFC266, white, 0.35) when the body's L* is above 70 or its hue is between 40° and 80°. Or add a thin darker sub-rim (fur × 0.85) inside the glow so the rim separates from warm fur.
- **[minor]** *03_golden_crop1/crop2 and zoom200: Puffmoss and the front creature rims* — The peach-fuzz rim wraps the full silhouette, including the bottom and the side facing away from the light, with equal intensity. It reads as a glow outline or sticker halo rather than directional backlight, which flattens the signature 'backlit felt' look.
  - **Fix:** In mossling_fur.gdshader, multiply rim by smoothstep(-0.2, 0.6, dot(normal_2d, -g_sun_dir.xy)) and add a ground-occlusion fade (rim *= smoothstep(0.0, 0.35, uv.y_from_bottom)). Keep the top and sun-facing edges at 0.8 and cut the bottom edge to 0.15 or less.
- **[minor]** *32_lineup_closeup_day_zoom200.png (Pebblepaw fur)* — At 200 % the fur grain is a blocky screen-space checker of about 6 px cells that reads like compression artefacts rather than fibres. A curator zooming into a store screenshot will notice it.
  - **Fix:** Switch the grain to object-anchored, rotated value noise with bilinear sampling and an anisotropic stretch along the fibre direction. Drop the grain contrast about 30 % on large flat creature areas, and add a blue-noise dither pass instead of quantised cells.
- **[major]** *32_lineup_closeup_day_zoom200 (Pebblepaw body); also visible at 1:1 in 32 on the lower body* — The felt and fur shading breaks into visible blocky 4-6 px squares, like a low-res noise texture magnified with nearest filtering. At close-up and portrait scale the creatures look pixelated, not soft felted. This weakens the handmade, touchable cuteness at the moment players look most closely.
  - **Fix:** In creature/mossling_fur.gdshader, sample the fibre noise with filter_linear_mipmap and repeat_enable. Alternatively, replace it with procedural object-space value noise at a frequency scaled by the instance's screen scale, so texel density stays at 1:1 or finer at 2.2x.
- **[minor]** *02_day / 03_golden: front Pebblepaw at (300, 1850) vs sun-stone group at (540, 1400)* — The front creature is the largest and brightest mass on the ground. It competes with the napping sun-stone group for focal, especially by day, so the eye splits between two cuddly targets.
  - **Fix:** Hold the front spot's depth scale about 8% lower, or drop its rim and key gain to 0.8 of the focal group. Give Puffmoss on the stone the top rim (1.0) so the sun stone stays the top local contrast in a blurred-greyscale check.
### systems

- **[major]** *drawcalls: Album sheet open 150-152, Garden sheet open 130-132 (limit 100)* — Opening a sheet adds 45-65 draw calls. The glass sheet and its cards break batching: each friend portrait or flower card looks like a separate glass_live sample plus a SubViewport-style portrait. This fails the budget and will hurt frame time on low-end devices.
  - **Fix:** While a sheet is fully open, freeze the world behind it: render it once to a blurred texture and pause the Parallax2D draw (it is about 88 calls hidden behind 85 % opaque glass). Use glass_faux, not glass_live, for the cards inside a sheet. Bake the album portraits into a single atlas texture once per session instead of rendering each live. Target 95 calls or fewer per open sheet.
- **[major]** *drawcalls/album.log (150-152) and drawcalls/garden_sheet.log (130-132), limit 100* — Draw calls run 30-50 % over budget with a sheet open, and 10-20 % above what the team reported. On mid-tier Android this will cost frame time exactly when glass and blur are active, and that hurts the M3-Expressive spring feel.
  - **Fix:** Batch the Album portrait thumbnails into one SubViewport atlas, or into baked ImageTextures that share one material. Share a single glass_faux material instance across all cards. Hide the world décor and props layers behind the sheet's opaque region (set visible=false on L4 props when the sheet is fully extended). Use CanvasGroup for each card's stack of rounded rect, label and hearts.
- **[minor]** *25_recap_dawn: second recap row* — 'Pip dropped by' with a paw icon reads as an unfinished sentence next to 'Dot visited for the first time'. It suggests a missing gift name, which slightly undercuts the warm-return (E3) moment. The recap scrim also dims the whole world to a flat grey-violet.
  - **Fix:** Fill the gift slot ('Pip dropped by with a dew cup'), or use a complete fallback string ('Pip stopped by to say hi'). Lighten the recap scrim to alpha 0.35 or less with a warm tint so the hollow stays visible behind the welcome card.
### garden

- **[major]** *All stills: flower beds and grass tufts across the meadow (for example 02_day y 1600-1850, 08 near foreground)* — The flora and grass tufts read as flat vector stickers, with uniform outlines, no fibre fringe and no soft lost edges. Next to the felted creatures and stones they look like a different hand, so the 'needle-felted diorama' look holds for the creatures only. The tufts are also scattered at an even density with no clustering, which is a programmer-art tell.
  - **Fix:** Have flora_bed.gdshader and grass.gdshader share the fibre-fringe edge function from inc/ (alpha fringe 2-3 px with noise), and add the object-anchored grain at 50 % of the creature strength. Re-place the tufts with Poisson-disk clusters of 3, 5 or 7 with ±12 % scale and ±5° rotation, and leave calm bare patches covering 30-40 % of the meadow.
- **[minor]** *06_day_rain: leaf umbrellas on the crest and focal creatures* — The leaf umbrellas are thin, outline-heavy and semi-transparent green shapes that barely read at phone size. The bible's eye path ('pale creatures under green umbrellas') does not land.
  - **Fix:** Fill the umbrella stamp with a solid fl_leaf body plus a lighter midrib, at about 1.25× scale with a 12° tilt. Add a small contact shade on the creature head beneath it and a rain-drip particle at the leaf tip.
### ui

- **[minor]** *19/20/22/24/28 sheets, text at y ≥ 1867; 21_sheet_album bottom row of cards* — Cards are clipped under the scroll fade and the dock: half-cut portraits and labels stay at full opacity right up to the fade. The contrast tool flags them (p5 1.0-1.7), and visually the fade band is too narrow, so the clip reads as an overlap bug rather than a scroll affordance.
  - **Fix:** Lengthen the bottom scroll fade to 96 px and end the sheet content area 24 px above the dock top. Exclude clipped or faded labels from the contrast sidecar by checking visible_ratio > 0.9, so the gate measures what users can actually see.
- **[major]** *22_album_friend_night.png and 25_recap_dawn.png: sheet/card surface vs dock* — At night the Album sheet and recap card are bright off-white panels sitting over a dark night-tinted dock and HUD. Two chrome themes appear in one frame, and the bright panel blows out the calm night mood. Against 2026 Liquid Glass this reads as a dated opaque modal.
  - **Fix:** Drive the sheet surface from the same night chrome token the dock uses (surface at night = dark glass, about 82 % alpha over blur). Alternatively keep the light surface but lerp the dock to light while a sheet is open. Make the recap card glass_live with a 24 dp blur and a 1 px top highlight, not an opaque #FAF8F2.
- **[major]** *26_vi_text200.png dock labels and dew chip* — At the 200 % text setting the dock labels and HUD grow only about 10-15 %. The Vietnamese labels fit, but text scaling is clearly capped, which falls short of WCAG and of the App Store accessibility-nutrition-label expectations.
  - **Fix:** Apply the text_scale multiplier to every Label theme font size, not only to the sheets. Let the dock grow to a two-line or icon-above-label layout with height min(96 dp × scale, 140 dp). Add a GUT test that asserts dock label font_size equals base × 2.0 at text200.
- **[minor]** *13_touch_tap_buttonbud_crop1 'Dot' name tag; 14/15 heart particles* — The name tag is mid-grey text on a translucent grey pill over green meadow, so it has low contrast and looks unstyled. The pet hearts are flat vector coral shapes with a white stroke and no grain. Both break the 'handmade felt' consistency and look like placeholder stickers.
  - **Fix:** Give the name tag surface_hi at 90 % with ink_1 text and a 2 dp drop shade tinted to the scene ambient. Render the hearts through particle_draw with the felt grain mask and a soft 1 dp edge falloff instead of a white stroke, and spring-scale them in from 0.6 to 1.0 (f 3 Hz, ζ 0.7).
- **[minor]** *25_recap_dawn 'Pip dropped by' line* — The row reads as truncated, as if a gift name is missing. It also uses the paw icon while the basket row carries the gift meaning, so the recap feels unfinished.
  - **Fix:** Check the recap string template ('{name} dropped by with {gift}'). If the gift is null on purpose, use 'Pip stopped by to say hi' so the line reads as complete.
- **[blocker]** *21_sheet_album (150-152 draw calls), 19/20_sheet_garden (130-132); limit 100* — Opening any sheet breaks the draw-call contract by 30-52%. On mid-range phones the most relaxing interaction, browsing friends, could start stuttering, which works directly against calm.
  - **Fix:** While a sheet covers more than 60% of the world, set visible=false on world CanvasItems fully under the sheet rect (L4 props and decor below y 650, L5 frame). Draw all album portrait cards from one atlas texture with one shared ShaderMaterial, and pass per-card data as instance uniforms so they batch. Draw glass_faux once per sheet, not per card.
- **[major]** *22_album_friend_night (tod 0.95): the sheet surface covers y 545-1665* — At night the sheet stays a near-white panel (about L* 92) over a world at about L* 10-20. That is a glaring jump for tired eyes in a dark bedroom, and it breaks the ambient night mood. The dock and HUD chips do switch to a dark night chrome, but the sheet does not.
  - **Fix:** Add a night sheet variant that follows the chrome switch already used in 27/29. Use a warm, dimmed surface at about L* 80-84, with ink kept at 7:1 or better, or a dark glass surface matching the night dock (#3A3F66-ish) with cream text. Cross-fade it over 600 ms on the tod change.
- **[major]** *26_vi_text200: dock labels and dew chip* — With text set to 200%, the dock labels grow only about 10%, and the icons actually shrink. Players who raise text size for night or low vision get almost nothing. This fails the intent of 'Reduce Motion/Transparency/Text work' under #8.
  - **Fix:** Apply the text-scale factor to the dock Label font_size through the theme type variation. Let the dock grow to 2 rows of label height, or hide icons only when the label would not fit at 200%. Add a GUT assert that the label's rendered height is at least 1.8x the 100% baseline.

## Strengths

- The creature design and fur rendering are on-brand. The fibre fringe and backlit rim on Puffmoss in 03_golden_zoom200 is the best thing in the build. Keep the SDF silhouette edge and the fringe rule.
- The night focal system works. The lantern pools on the far bank, their streaks in the brook, the glow mushrooms at the stone and the fireflies give 05/09 a calm, readable eye path with a compressed value ladder, as the bible intends.
- The UI is clean and consistent: one radius family, frosted glass that stays readable over busy grass, a confident primary blue, and crisp text at 1:1. Album cards with live portraits and the recap overlay look premium.
- The rain state (06) is coherent. Flattened palette, leaf umbrellas only on creatures in the open, and soft cloud banks: it reads as a mood, not a filter.
- The depth ladder is good by day and at dusk. Far hills haze toward sky_horizon and the treeline, crest, meadow and L5 frame step forward in value, so there are at least 5 planes.
- The night lantern frame (05) is a genuine key-art moment: warm lantern pools with streaks doubled in the brook, glowing mushrooms at the sun stone and fireflies. It hits the §1.3 eye path and would hold up against Cats & Soup or Usagi Shima night shots.
- The creature design and felted fur read well: baby-schema proportions, consistent species silhouettes, blush and fringe. Crop1 of the sun stone trio works as a 1:1 store tile at golden and at dusk.
- Atmospheric depth is convincing: six planes, with haze lifting the far hills and tree line toward the horizon colour at every time of day. The dusk sky with pink clouds is especially strong.
- The UI typography and component language are clean and current: rounded display type, a confident single primary blue, pill segmented controls, a basket progress ring with a badge, and good localisation fit (Vietnamese).
- Ambient motion is alive but calm: about 6.5 % of pixels move per frame, every region moves, blinks and breathing show on the focal trio, and nothing pops.
- The creatures are strongly baby-schema: large low-set eyes with double catch-lights, blush cheeks, round bodies, tiny grass at their feet, and the closed-eye sleeping variants. They are genuinely irresistible, and the pet-hold squash with closed eyes and floating hearts (14/15) is a delightful payoff.
- The night frames (05/09/29) are the best mood in the set. The palette is compressed blue with warm lantern reflections doubling in the brook, glow mushrooms at the sun stone, fireflies, and a dark night chrome on the dock and HUD. Calm and readable without glare (except the sheets).
- Day, dawn and dusk palettes are soft and low-chroma, with clear depth across sky, far hills, treeline, meadow, brook and fg frame. Roughly 40% of the frame is calm sky, so the scene breathes and never feels cluttered.
- V1 holds: five independently moving ambient layers (sky, willow, crest, focal, brook), with no shake and no flashes, and the motion strip shows gentle continuous change.
- The UI type is large, high-contrast and friendly, with rounded glass sheets, a clear selected state in the dock, and warm plain-language copy ('Moss and flowers fill it, drop by drop'). No guilt or neglect visuals anywhere, so E3 holds visually.
