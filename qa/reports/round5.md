## Issues by owner

### env

- **[blocker]** *05_night.png / 05_night_crop2: under the far rock, stump, basket, tree base and sun stone (approx y 1050-1250 and 1380 at 1080 width)* — contact_shadow renders as saturated electric-blue pools (reads like spilled water) at night. That breaks the compressed night value ladder, competes with the warm lantern focal system and puts the highest chroma in the frame on the ground. It is the main reason lighting coherence is below 7.
  - **Fix:** In contact_shadow.gdshader, stop deriving shadow colour from a chroma-boosted ambient. Use shadow_rgb = mix(ground_rgb, ground_rgb * g_ambient, 0.55), multiplicative, never additive or lerped toward a pure sky blue. Clamp OKLCh chroma to <= ground chroma + 0.02 and cap alpha at 0.45 for night tod. Add a metrics check that night high-chroma blue area under props is < 0.5 %.
- **[major]** *03_golden.png / 07: central cluster around the sun stone (y 1150-1400)* — The golden haze and shaft veil wash the sun stone and the napping frog into a low-contrast pinkish-olive soup, and the stone itself looks translucent. The focal (the creature on the stone) has less local contrast than the white-rimmed front creature and the basket, so the bible's eye path, sun to stone to brook, does not read.
  - **Fix:** Mask the air/haze layer with a vertical falloff so its alpha drops to <= 0.05 below y 1100. Give the sun stone an opaque prop_tone material with a darker shade side (L* about 45) so the creature sits on a value base. Lift the stone creature's rim and exposure +8 L*, and drop the front creature's exposure 5 L*, so the focal wins the blurred-greyscale test.
- **[blocker]** *05_night.png / 05_night_crop2.png: under the far rock (~700,1230 in source px), the stump base (~900,1400), the basket (~500,1450) and the willow/tree base (~120,1300)* — contact_shadow.gdshader leaves saturated electric-blue pools under every prop at night. They read as puddles of paint, are the highest-chroma area in the frame, and pull the eye away from the lantern focal. A curator would reject a night screenshot on this alone.
  - **Fix:** In contact_shadow.gdshader, stop blending the shadow toward the ambient colour at night. Compute the shadow as a multiply toward a darkened ground tone: shadow_rgb = ground_rgb * mix(1.0, 0.72, strength), with chroma clamped to the OKLCh C of the night meadow (≤0.06). Fade the alpha to ≤0.35 at tod>0.85. Add a metrics check: a high-chroma pixel share > 1 % inside prop footprint masks fails the night still.
- **[major]** *03_golden.png sun stone under Puffmoss (~540,1300 source px); visible in motion_detail_strip* — The sun stone at golden renders as a pale, semi-transparent pink slab. The grass and mushroom texture seem to show through it and its silhouette dissolves into the light shafts, so the one focal object the bible calls for (#DCB998, highest local contrast) reads as a ghost. Squinted, the focal is the basket or the sky, not the stone and creature.
  - **Fix:** Draw the light shafts and haze veil behind the sun stone (z below the focal props), or mask them with the stone alpha. Give the stone an opaque albedo of #DCB998 with a darker #8F6E5E contact underside so the creature-on-stone group holds the top local-contrast tile in the squint test.
- **[major]** *All main-scene stills: the top ~38 % of the 2400 px frame (y 0–900), the sky* — Composition is bottom-heavy. Almost half the portrait frame is empty gradient sky with soft clouds, so a random 1:1 crop has roughly a 40 % chance of being blank sky. The Today-tab key-art test fails, and next to Tiny Glade or Usagi Shima the hero frame feels zoomed out and under-populated.
  - **Fix:** Bring the horizon up to ~30 % of the frame (sky 0–720). Fill the upper third with intentional elements: the willow canopy entering from the left down to y≈500, a hanging vine or bunting with chimes from the top-right frond, and drifting dandelion seeds and birds. Or push the camera 10 % closer, so the creature cluster sits on the lower third line (y≈1600) and the sun sits on the upper third.
- **[minor]** *05_night.png creatures (front Pebblepaw, stump Primpip, Honeyhum)* — At night the creatures stay close to their day colours (warm orange and yellow) and have no visible #9DB4FF moon rim on their upper-right edges, so they read pasted-on against the compressed blue ladder.
  - **Fix:** Apply the ambient multiply #5D6AA0 to the creature albedo at tod>0.85, then add the moon rim (α 0.5, using the upper-right edge mask from the golden rim fix). Keep a warm bounce only on the side facing a lantern pool.
- **[blocker]** *05_night.png / 05_night_crop2.png: under the rock (~700,1230), stump (~900,1400), basket, tree base (~60,1290) and sun stone rim* — Contact shadows render as saturated electric-blue pools (high chroma, brighter than the night meadow). This is the single most jarring thing in the set: the shadows look lit and wet, they break the warm-lantern focal system, they draw the tired eye to the wrong places and they violate the tinted-not-glowing shadow rule.
  - **Fix:** In contact_shadow.gdshader, compute the shadow as a multiply toward the shadow tint (mix(1.0, g_shadow_tint, a)) instead of adding or alpha-blending the tint. At night clamp the tint to OKLCh C ≤ 0.04 and make it darker than the ground (L* −6 to −10 versus meadow #1C274D). Add a QA assert that the shadow region's L* is less than the surrounding ground L* in every ToD.
- **[major]** *03_golden_crop1 / 07 crop1 / motion_detail_strip: sun stone under Puffmoss (~540,1300 at 1:1)* — The sun stone at golden reads as a translucent pink-lilac disc that ghosts over Puffmoss's lower body, and the creature's legs and belly dissolve into it. The focal creature looks washed out and semi-transparent instead of the highest-contrast, backlit point. It also looks like a render error.
  - **Fix:** Draw the light-shaft/air layer below creature z, or mask it with the creature alpha. Reduce the stone's halo/haze add to α ≤ 0.08 at golden. Make sure the stone's draw order is strictly behind the creature (y-sort origin at the creature's foot rather than the stone's centre). Then re-run the squint test so the stone and creature hold the max local-contrast tile.
- **[minor]** *08/09/11 lineup backdrops; 12_plush_closeup* — The lineup background is a flat mint green by day and muddy olive at golden. That makes the creature sheet read as a test harness, and the olive is a slightly sad, sickly tone.
  - **Fix:** Give the lineup a soft vertical gradient from sky_horizon to meadow, with a 25 % vignette and a faint ground plane. At golden, mix the ground toward #AD8773 instead of multiplying green by the golden key.
### creature

- **[major]** *All scene stills, creature silhouettes (03_golden_zoom200, 07 zoom200, 05_night_zoom200)* — Every creature has a uniform bright 2-4 px light outline on all sides, including the sun-averse bottom and left edges at golden and the moon-averse edges at night. It reads as a sticker cut-out halo, not the directional peach-fuzz backlight rim the bible calls for, and it contradicts g_sun_dir.
  - **Fix:** In mossling_fur.gdshader, weight the rim by saturate(dot(edge_normal_2d, -g_sun_dir.xy)) * rim + 0.15 base. Colour it with g_key (golden #FFC266, night moon #9DB4FF at alpha 0.5) and suppress it on the ground-contact third (uv.y > 0.66). Replace the flat outline with fibre-noise-broken alpha so the edge reads as felt fuzz, as in the plush reference the user asked for.
- **[major]** *12_plush_closeup.png and the lineup sheets: body surface of Puffmoss and Honeyhum* — The bodies read as smooth matte vinyl or clay with soft gradients. There is almost no felted fibre texture or surface fuzz except a faint fringe at the top silhouette, so they fall short of the 'needle-felted plush' look and the plush-toy reference quality. The underside shading also goes grey-teal, which looks muddy.
  - **Fix:** Add a tiling fibre-noise normal or value perturbation in the fur shader: 2 octaves at 6 px and 2 px scale, amplitude 0.04-0.06 in L*, aligned to a radial flow. Extend fringe hairs along the whole silhouette, 3-5 px, density weighted by rim. Tint the underside shade toward the warm ground bounce (mix 25 % ground_rgb) instead of neutral grey-teal. Add a subtle 1-2 % specular sheen on the top lobe.
- **[minor]** *08/09/11 lineup sheets backdrop* — The lineup backdrop is a flat green field by day and olive at golden. Without ground variation, creatures do not separate from it (the cream and green species lose edges), and golden reads muddy.
  - **Fix:** Use a neutral warm-grey backdrop (#D8D2C6 day, palette-driven multiply) with a soft vignette and per-creature contact ellipse, so species colour is judged against a neutral.
- **[major]** *03_golden.png / 07_golden_full_hideui.png: creature silhouettes in the cluster and the front Pebblepaw (03_golden_zoom200)* — The signature beat, the backlit peach-fuzz rim in #FFC266 at golden hour, is missing. Rims are a thin, near-white even halo all the way round the body, so the creatures look like sticker cutouts rather than felt lit from behind. The plush close-up (12) shows good fibre fringe by day, but at golden that fringe does not glow.
  - **Fix:** Drive the rim colour from palette key_rim (#FFC266) and weight it by dot(edge_normal, -g_sun_dir): sun-facing and top edges get rim 0.8 and backlight 1.0, the shade side gets 0.1. Widen the fringe noise to 3–4 px at golden only and add the rim additively after the ambient multiply, so it reaches L* 90+.
- **[major]** *08–11 lineup stills, background* — The lineup backdrop is a flat green field by day and a muddy olive at golden, with a vignette. It is the only surface in the game that does not look handmade, the creatures float with heavy dark drop shadows, and these frames are what the studio would ship as character marketing. The Gemini-style plush showcase the user pointed to wins on its staged, soft set.
  - **Fix:** Replace the flat colour with a staged felt set: a soft cream to sky gradient (an OKLab blend of palette sky_horizon and key), a felted ground disc (the same flora_bed grain and fibre noise) under each creature and a 2-stop vignette. Use the ambient-tinted contact shadow, not a black-alpha ellipse, and keep the golden backdrop peach (#FFCB7D → #E8A3A1) rather than olive.
- **[major]** *All scenes and lineups, e.g. 03_golden_zoom200, 09_lineup_golden_crop1* — The creature rim is a uniform pale outline all the way round, including the shadow side and bottom. It reads as a sticker cut-out halo rather than the signature directional peach-fuzz backlight, and it flattens the plush volume. At golden the rim is not #FFC266 on the sun side.
  - **Fix:** In mossling_fur.gdshader, weight the rim by dot(normal2d, -g_sun_dir) (smoothstep 0.1–0.7) and tint it g_key/rim_color per ToD. Drop the rim on the away-from-sun and lower 30 % of the silhouette to ≤ 0.15. Keep a ≤ 1 px soft AA edge there instead of a light halo.
- **[major]** *12_plush_closeup.png, 15_newfriend_crop1, 14 portrait* — For the plush look the user asked for: the bodies read as smooth matte vinyl or clay with only a sparse fibre fringe on the top edge. There is no felt micro-texture, no soft lost edge and no fuzz scatter. Compared with the premium plush-creature references, it is missing the tactile 'I want to squeeze it' cue.
  - **Fix:** Add an object-anchored felt noise to fur shading (2 octaves, ±4 % L*, fine scale ≈ 3 px plus a 12 px clump). Extend the fibre fringe around the full silhouette (density ×2, length 3–6 px, alpha falloff) with a subtle subsurface warm bounce on the belly (mix 0.15 of cr_cream). Add a soft fake AO under the cheeks and ears. Keep the eyes as they are.
- **[minor]** *06_day_rain_crop1: leaf umbrellas* — Leaf umbrellas are faint and semi-transparent and partly hidden behind heads. The rain eye-path cue ('pale creatures under green umbrellas') does not read, and the creatures look like they are being rained on, which feels slightly sad.
  - **Fix:** Render the umbrella leaf at α 1.0 using fl_leaf, at L* about 15 below the fur, tilted 12°, sitting above the head with a 1-px darker midrib. Give it its own contact shadow on the head.
### garden

- **[major]** *07_golden_full_hideui.png, front creature at ~(250,1530) display / zoom200* — Shepherd's-hook lantern posts draw on top of the front creature's body and face, which is a y-sort or z-index error. It destroys the focal read of the hero creature.
  - **Fix:** Put lantern decor in the same YSort as creatures, sorted by its base anchor y. Alternatively, have the decor anchor allocator reject any anchor within the creature spot radius plus 40 px of an occupied creature spot. Add a GUT test that asserts no decor sprite rect overlaps a creature rect when the decor is drawn later.
- **[major]** *07_golden_full_hideui.png and its zoom200: lantern posts at x≈130–360, y≈1780–1900 source px, over the front Pebblepaw* — Two lantern shepherd-hooks draw in front of the front creature's face and body. This is a y-sort error on the most important foreground subject, and it ruins exactly the 1:1 crop a curator would use as key art.
  - **Fix:** Y-sort decor by foot position and use the same sort key as creatures: put DecorLayer and the creature layer under one y_sort_enabled parent, with offsets at the base of each post. Also add a placement exclusion radius (≥90 px) round creature wander anchors so posts are never generated inside a creature's footprint.
- **[major]** *07_golden_full_hideui_crop1: lantern posts at x≈0–140, y≈1700–1900 overlap the front creature* — Firefly-lantern posts draw over the front mossling's face and body. It reads as cluttered and as the creature being caged, which is the opposite of cozy.
  - **Fix:** Set the y-sort origin of lantern decor to the post base, and add the creature's footprint (radius ≈ 90 px) as an exclusion zone in the DecorLayer anchor picker. If an anchor still overlaps, fade the decor to α 0.35 within 60 px of a creature's bbox.
### ui

- **[major]** *18_garden_vi200.png (card row y~2366 under the dock) and 15_newfriend dock labels and 'Say hello' button* — At 200 % text in Vietnamese, sheet content runs under the dock with 1.08-1.51 contrast and gets clipped. In the new-friend modal, the dimmed dock labels (2.29-4.41) and the 'Say hello' button (4.39) fail WCAG AA.
  - **Fix:** Have the sheet ScrollContainer bottom inset = dock height + safe area, with a fade mask, and let the card grow vertically at large text sizes. Hide or set the dock to inert with labels removed from contrast scope while a modal is up, or keep labels at text_primary on the scrim. Darken the button blue fill by ~6 % L (e.g. #3653B8) or use 600-weight white text to reach >= 4.5:1.
- **[minor]** *No interaction captures* — Touch feedback cannot be verified: there are no tap, press-state or haptic frames, and no video with sheets opening.
  - **Fix:** Capture a 60 fps clip of taps on each dock button, a creature and a flower bed, with a frame counter. Verify that the first visual change happens within 6 frames.
- **[major]** *15_newfriend.png dock (y≈2050–2250 source px); also 13_album and 16_postcards dock labels* — With the modal open, the dock is dimmed unevenly. 'Settings' stays at full contrast while Garden, Basket and Album fade to 2.3–4.4:1, which looks like a bug (Settings reads as selected). The 'Say hello' button sits at 4.39:1, below AA, and is the one confident-blue CTA.
  - **Fix:** Put one full-screen scrim (α 0.35 of the ink token) above the dock and below the modal, instead of fading items one by one. Darken the CTA blue by about 6 % L (toward #2F4FB8) or set its label weight to 700 at 18 sp so it reaches ≥4.5:1. Shift the contrast-measure box 8 px inside the dock edge so the 13 and 16 failures are not false positives.
- **[minor]** *18_garden_vi200.png, card rows below y≈2366 and the dock* — At 200 % text, the sheet content runs below the fold under the dock, and the card row there measures 1.08–1.51:1. The dock reflows to a 2×2 grid that overlaps the sheet's bottom edge.
  - **Fix:** Make the sheet body a ScrollContainer and inset its bottom by the dock height plus 16 dp. Above 150 % text, switch the dock to icon-only plus a selected label instead of a 2×2 reflow.
- **[major]** *18_garden_vi200.png bottom (y≈1900–2260) and 14/13/16 dock inactive labels* — At 200 % Vietnamese text the dock wraps to 2 rows, and the inactive items 'Giỏ quà' and 'Album' are pale grey on frosted glass (about 2–3:1). The card row runs under the dock below the fold. At night (14) the inactive Garden/Basket labels are grey-on-navy and hard to read for tired eyes. This fails WCAG AA.
  - **Fix:** Use ink_2 or a darker colour for inactive dock labels on light glass, and #C9CFEA or lighter on night glass, for ≥ 4.5:1. At text scale ≥ 150 % switch the dock to icon-plus-short-label or a horizontal scroll, never 2 rows. Add a bottom safe padding to the sheet scroll container equal to the dock height + 24 dp so the last card scrolls clear.
- **[minor]** *15_newfriend.png dock behind the modal, plus the 'Say hello' button* — Dock labels behind the scrim measure 2.29–4.41 and the button 4.39. V2 fails at 77.1 % because the full-bleed pastel card and scrim area push saturation.
  - **Fix:** Dim the dock to α 0.4 under modal scrims and exclude it from the contrast gate as an inert element, or blur it. Darken the button fill to primary_pressed #3A5DC2 to reach ≥ 4.5:1 with white text. Lower the card hero gradient saturation to S ≤ 0.35.
### systems

- **[minor]** *capture.gd / motion_clip_golden.mp4 first ~2 s* — Boot settle changes up to 88.6 % of pixels per frame. Any player-facing launch or resume shows the pop, and there is no pre-roll to hide it.
  - **Fix:** Hold a full-screen cover tinted with the palette sky colour, then fade it out over 0.6 s once the settle metric drops below 5 % changed pixels or after 50 frames. Add a --preroll flag to capture.gd.
- **[minor]** *motion_clip_golden.mp4, frames 0–50 (boot settle, up to 88.6 % of pixels change)* — Every launch pops: palettes, shadows and springs visibly snap into place over about 1.7 s. This breaks the 'no onset without a fade' rule, and any store preview video recorded from a cold start shows it.
  - **Fix:** Settle the simulation off-screen: tick springs and ambient tweens for 60 frames with the canvas hidden behind a 400 ms ease-out fade from the sky_horizon colour. Add a --qa-preroll=N flag to capture.gd so QA clips start steady.
- **[minor]** *motion_framediff.json, frames 0–50 (boot settle)* — For the first ~1.7 s up to 88.6 % of pixels change between frames. If that also happens on app resume it is a jarring pop, which goes against E3/V5 ('no onset without a fade'). Touch feedback could not be verified at all.
  - **Fix:** Add a 400 ms cross-fade from the splash or last frame on boot and resume. Add a --qa-preroll flag to capture.gd. Next round, capture tap-response frames for each control (≤ 6 frames).

## Strengths

- Night lantern system (05_night_crop3) is genuinely premium: warm pools, vertical streak reflections in the brook and fireflies give a clear warm-vs-cool night focal. Preserve it.
- Value-layered depth: sky, far hills, tree line, meadow, brook and L5 foreground vignette read as 6 planes with haze, especially in 07 and dawn.
- Creature face design (eye size, specular dots, blush, tiny mouths) is charming and consistent across all 12 species and both poses, and holds up crisply at 200 %.
- Motion has at least 5 decorrelated ambient layers, plus breathing, sway and blinks. It is calm and never static.
- UI type and frosted card chrome are clean and consistent, and the MSDF-quality text stays crisp at 200 %; the album portraits are especially nice.
- The plush creature design is genuinely premium: soft rounded felt masses, fibre fringe, glossy two-highlight eyes and blush (12_plush_closeup). It is competitive with Cats & Soup and Usagi Shima character appeal, and the 12-species lineup shows clear silhouette variety.
- Night lantern pools with streak reflections doubling in the brook (05_night_crop3_lanterns) are the bible's signature night beat, delivered with warm and cool contrast. Keep them.
- Motion is calm and layered: ≥5 independent ambient layers, low per-frame change (~3.5 %), a breathing and blink cadence, and no static frames. It feels alive without busyness.
- The UI has a coherent frosted high-opacity look: rounded tokenised radii, one confident blue, clean text and icons at 200 % zoom, and Vietnamese diacritics rendered cleanly at 200 % text.
- The time-of-day palettes are distinct and harmonious (dawn lilac, day sky, golden peach, rain desaturated green), and rain tucks creatures under leaf umbrellas, which is charming storytelling.
- Night composition (05) is genuinely soothing: the lantern pools along the brook, their reflected streaks, the glow mushrooms at the sun stone, the moon, sparse stars and dim fireflies give a warm focal at a low brightness that is comfortable for tired eyes. Keep this once the blue shadows are fixed.
- The baby-schema faces are irresistible: large low-set eyes with two highlights, a small mouth, cheek blush and rounded silhouettes. The sleeping-crescent eye variants and the blink and breathing in the detail strip read as alive yet calm.
- The golden-hour palette is warm, pastel and low-arousal. The sun sitting in the hill gap with its haze band matches the reference frame, and V2 calm share is 90–91 %.
- Ambient motion is layered and gentle: at least 5 independent regions (clouds, canopy and chimes, meadow, creatures, flowers, brook), no static frames, and about 3.5 % of pixels moving per frame. Nothing moves fast.
- The frosted UI chrome is soft and rounded, with friendly type and a well-themed night variant. The friend and new-friend portrait cards present the creatures like collectible plush.
